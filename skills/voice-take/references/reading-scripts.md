# Reading scripts for voice takes

Each script is about 60–70 words, around 25 seconds at a natural pace. Each one asks for a different delivery, so a few takes together cover the range a narrator needs. Numbers are spelled out so the transcript matches what is said.

## How to record

- Same microphone and room as the videos the voice will narrate, somewhere quiet and without echo.
- Read the script once silently, then record it in one go at the pace you want in your videos. Don't perform harder than you would on camera.
- Leave about half a second of silence before and after.
- If you stumble, start the whole script again rather than patching a line. One script per file.
- A voice memo on a phone is fine if that's what your videos use.

## 1. Calm explainer (`calm-explainer`)

> Most people never think about how a simple object is designed. It starts with a problem, something small that gets in the way every single day. Then you sketch, measure, and test, over and over, until the shape feels obvious. That's the part nobody sees. The finished piece looks simple because all of the hard decisions were made long before you ever picked it up.

## 2. Upbeat hook (`upbeat-hook`)

> Okay, this one honestly surprised me. I wanted something quick, cheap, and actually useful, and I did not expect it to work this well. It clicks together in seconds, it holds up to real use, and it looks great on the desk. Stick around, because I'm going to show you exactly how it's made, what I'd change, and where you can grab it yourself.

## 3. Step-by-step with numbers (`walkthrough`)

> Here's how the build comes together. First, measure the space you're working with; mine was about thirty-two centimetres wide. Next, print the base at twenty percent infill, which takes roughly three hours. Then attach the two side arms, and check that they sit flat. If anything wobbles, sand the edges lightly, or flip the part over and try again. That's really all there is to it.

## 4. Conversational, with questions (`conversational`)

> So, why bother making your own instead of just buying one? Fair question. Sometimes you can't find the right size. Sometimes the store version is flimsy, or overpriced, or just ugly. And sometimes, if I'm being honest, it's simply more fun to build it. Have you ever bought something and thought, I could have done that better? Yeah. Me too. That's where this started.

## 5. Closing and call to action (`closing-cta`)

> That's it for this one. If it helped, the files are free and linked down below, so you can print your own tonight. Let me know what you'd improve, because a lot of my best changes come straight from your comments. And if you want more practical builds like this, follow along. I'll see you in the next video.

## Writing a custom script

When the user's videos are about something else, write one in the same shape:
- 55–75 words of complete sentences;
- one clear delivery (calm, upbeat, step-by-step, conversational, closing);
- the user's real vocabulary: product names, units, phrases they actually say;
- numbers and symbols spelled out;
- at least one question and one list, so the take carries rising and falling intonation.
