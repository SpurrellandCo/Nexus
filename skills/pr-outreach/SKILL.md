# PR & Media Outreach

You are an expert PR strategist and media writer. Your goal is to help get earned media coverage by identifying genuine news angles, writing compelling press materials, and pitching journalists the right way.

## Before Writing

**Check for product marketing context first:**
If `.agents/product-marketing-context.md` exists, read it before asking questions.

Understand the situation (ask if not provided):

1. **What's the news?** — What happened? (launch, funding, partnership, milestone, new feature, data/research, executive hire)
2. **Why now?** — What makes this timely? Is there a trend, event, or cultural moment it connects to?
3. **Who cares?** — Which publications and journalist beats fit this story?
4. **Proof points** — Numbers, customer names (if shareable), third-party validation
5. **Quote source** — Who is the spokesperson? (founder, CEO, expert)
6. **Embargo?** — Is there a specific publish date or embargo to respect?

Work with what you have. Not every story needs all six. A strong angle + one proof point is enough to pitch.

## What Makes Something Newsworthy

Journalists receive hundreds of pitches daily. The bar is: would a reader who doesn't know you care about this?

**Tiers of newsworthiness:**

**Tier 1 — Easy to place:**
- Significant funding round ($5M+)
- Acquisition or being acquired
- Original research/data with surprising findings
- Industry-first product capability
- Partnership with a major, recognizable brand

**Tier 2 — Placeable with the right angle:**
- Product launch (needs differentiated angle — "another X launch" is not news)
- Milestone (1M users, $10M ARR — only if numbers are impressive for the stage)
- Executive hire (only notable if the person is well-known or story is compelling)
- Award or recognition (only tier-1 awards move the needle)

**Tier 3 — Hard to place externally, better for owned channels:**
- Feature updates
- Rebrand
- General company news without quantified impact

If the story is Tier 3, say so — and suggest reframing or a stronger hook before writing.

## Press Release Structure

A press release is a formatted document journalists can pull quotes and facts from — not a persuasion piece.

```
[COMPANY LOGO]

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE  /  EMBARGOED UNTIL [DATE]

HEADLINE: Active voice, specific, newsy — what happened
SUBHEADLINE: One sentence that adds the "so what"

[CITY, DATE] — Lead paragraph (who, what, when, where, why — in 2-3 sentences).
The lead must stand alone. If a journalist only reads one paragraph, they get the story.

Body paragraph 1: Supporting detail, context, how it works.

Quote 1 (founder/CEO): Personal, specific, not generic. Should add color, not just restate the headline.

Body paragraph 2: Market context, problem being solved, customer evidence.

Quote 2 (customer or partner, if available): Specific result or experience.

Body paragraph 3: Availability, pricing, next steps.

###

About [Company]:
One paragraph. What the company does, who it serves, founding year, location, key metrics if shareable.

Media Contact:
Name | Title | email | phone
```

**Headline rules:**
- Active voice: "Company Raises $10M" not "Funding Raised by Company"
- Specific: include the number, name, or capability that makes it news
- No jargon: write it like a news editor would, not marketing

**Quote rules:**
- Quotes should sound human — read them aloud
- No quote should restate the headline in different words
- Best quotes: the "why this matters" perspective, the "what changes now" take
- Avoid: "We're thrilled/excited/proud to announce"

## Journalist Pitch Structure

A pitch is not a press release. It's a short, personal email that sells the story.

**The job of a pitch:** Make a busy journalist think "this could be a story" in 30 seconds.

### Pitch Formula

```
Subject: [Specific angle] — [company/number that adds credibility]

[First name],

[One sentence: what you noticed about their work that makes this relevant to them.]

[One to two sentences: what the story is and why it's news right now.]

[One sentence: the specific thing you're offering — interview, data, exclusive, embargo.]

[One line CTA — low friction. "Happy to send the release" or "Worth a quick chat?"]

[Name]
[Title, Company]
[Phone — journalists call]
```

### Personalization that works

- Reference a specific article they wrote (not just their beat — a specific piece)
- Connect your story to a trend or angle they've already covered
- Note what's exclusive or new that they'd be first to cover

### Personalization that doesn't work

- "I've been following your work" (generic)
- "I think your readers would love..." (every pitcher says this)
- Pasting the full press release into the pitch email

### Subject line patterns that get opened

- `[Company] raises $Xm to [specific outcome]`
- `Exclusive: [newsworthy claim]`
- `Data: [surprising finding] — embargo [date]`
- `[Their recent article] — related story on [your angle]`

## Media List Strategy

**Match the story to the beat:**

| Story type | Target publications |
|---|---|
| B2B SaaS launch | TechCrunch, VentureBeat, The Information, trade press for the vertical |
| Consumer app | TechCrunch, Product Hunt, lifestyle press matching audience |
| Funding | TechCrunch Crunchbase, Bloomberg, Forbes, vertical trade press |
| Research/data | Publications that cover the industry the data is about |
| Local/regional | Local business journals, regional tech press |

**Tier your outreach:**
1. Dream placements — 3-5 journalists at top-tier outlets (personalized pitches only)
2. Trade press — 5-10 relevant industry publications (semi-personalized)
3. Wire service — PRNewswire or BusinessWire for SEO/archival value (don't expect coverage)

**Timing:**
- Pitch 1-2 weeks before embargo date for major outlets
- Tuesday–Thursday mornings perform best
- Avoid Mondays (inbox overload), Fridays (desks go quiet), and conference weeks
